*The Scrum Guide prescribes no format. The structure below follows the five stages from Derby and Larsen's* Agile Retrospectives *(2006), which remain the most useful because each stage prevents a specific failure.*

---

## Prime Directive
*Read it aloud. Do not link to it.*

> Regardless of what we discover, we understand and truly believe that everyone did the best job they could, given what they knew at the time, their skills and abilities, the resources available, and the situation at hand.
>
> Norm Kerth, *Project Retrospectives*, 2001

*Thirty seconds, every time, including when the team knows it. It sets the one condition under which people will name a real problem: that doing so costs them nothing.*

*This is not ceremony. Google's Project Aristotle found psychological safety the strongest of five dynamics separating effective teams from ineffective ones across more than 180 teams, and it is the condition this event depends on entirely.*

---

## 1. Set the stage

*Get everyone to speak in the first two minutes. Someone who has not spoken by minute five usually will not.*

*A one-word check-in works. So does asking each person for a single word describing the Sprint. The mechanism is participation, not the question.*

*State the focus if there is one, and the timebox.*

---

## 2. Gather data

*What actually happened, before anyone interprets it.*

*Facts first, deliberately. Skipping to opinions lets the loudest memory become the team's history, and the quiet problem never gets mentioned.*

*Sources: the Sprint Goal and whether it was met, the [changes log](sprint-backlog.md) from the Sprint, incidents, what was learned at the [review](sprint-review-notes.md), the emotional timeline of the Sprint.*

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| | |

**How it felt.** *Ask for the low points and the high points. Feelings are data about a system: the day everyone was frustrated is pointing at something real.*

---

## 3. Generate insights

*Why did it happen that way? Look for patterns and causes, not fixes.*

*Prefer "how" questions to "why" questions. "How was it possible for that to reach production?" produces a system answer. "Why did you do that?" produces a person, and stops the analysis one step early.*

*Check the last few retrospectives. A problem recurring for the third time is not the same problem; it is evidence that your previous fixes addressed a symptom.*

---

## 4. Decide what to do

*One or two changes. Not five.*

*The separate stage exists because meetings otherwise end in agreement and nothing else. Agreement is not a decision.*

*A change without an owner and a tracking number is not scheduled, and unscheduled work does not happen. This is how retrospectives decay: not through bad discussion, but through good discussion that changes nothing, which teaches everyone the meeting is theatre.*

| Change | Owner | Ticket | How we will know it worked |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |

*Prefer changes the team controls outright. An action depending on another team's cooperation belongs to whoever will actually chase it, and if nobody will, do not write it down.*

**Last retrospective's actions.** *Check them here, first thing. Anything undone gets a decision now: do it, drop it, or escalate it. Carrying items silently is what teaches a team that the list is optional.*

| Previous action | Status | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| | *Done \| Dropped \| Carried, with a reason* | |

---

## 5. Close

*Sixty seconds. Confirm the actions and their owners, then ask one question about the retrospective itself: was this worth the hour?*

*Ending deliberately matters more than it sounds. It marks the decisions as decisions, and it gives the facilitator the only feedback available on whether the format is still working.*

## Notes on using this template

*Delete this section too.*

**Vary the format, keep the stages.** Running the same three columns every Sprint produces the same three answers. The five stages are the structure; how you gather data or generate insights within them should change.

**One or two actions.** A retrospective producing eight actions produces zero, and it costs the team its belief that the event does anything.

**If nobody raises a real problem, that is the finding.** It means the team does not yet believe the room is safe, and no format fixes that. Change who facilitates, change who attends, or address it directly. A retrospective where everything was fine, in a Sprint where things clearly were not, is measuring safety rather than performance.

**Managers.** If a line manager attends, expect the honest content to disappear, however well-intentioned they are. If they must be there, it is on them to speak least and to visibly act on what they hear.

**Where this lives:** the wiki, keeping the series together so patterns are visible. Actions go to the tracker, always. If your team reliably moves actions to the tracker, you can skip the write-up entirely and keep only the action list; the discussion is the product, not the page.
