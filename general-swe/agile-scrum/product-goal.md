*One Product Goal at a time. The Scrum Guide is explicit: a team "must fulfill (or abandon) one objective before taking on the next." Two goals is no goal, because the moment they compete, ordering the backlog stops having a criterion again.*

---

## The goal

*One sentence describing a future state of the product. Not a feature, not a project, not a list.*

*Write the state, not the work. "Launch the mobile app" is work and stops meaning anything the day it ships. "Customers can complete a purchase on a phone in under two minutes" is a state, and it stays a useful target while the work to reach it changes.*

> **Example.** Small merchants can onboard and take their first payment the same day, without contacting support.

---

## Why this, now

*Two or three sentences. What changes for the business or the user if you reach it, and what made it the next thing rather than a later thing.*

*This is what people cite when they argue for an item. Without it, backlog ordering falls back to whoever asked most recently.*

---

## How we will know

*The measures that tell you the goal is reached, with where each is measured and what it is today.*

*You do not need many. One or two that move only if the goal is genuinely achieved beat six that are easy to collect.*

| Measure | Today | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Merchants taking a first payment within 24h of signup* | *18%* | *60%* | *Onboarding funnel dashboard* |

*Be honest about lagging measures. If a number will not move for three months, say so, and name a leading indicator you will watch meanwhile.*

## Notes on using this template

*Delete this section too.*

**Abandoning is a legitimate outcome and the Guide says so.** A goal you can only leave by achieving is a commitment you cannot learn from. Decide in advance what evidence would make you drop it.

**Keep it visible where ordering happens.** The Product Goal exists to make backlog ordering decidable. Sitting in a document nobody opens, it cannot do that. Pin it at the top of the backlog in your tracker and let this page hold the reasoning.

**One page maximum.** A Product Goal that needs more than a page has become a plan, and a plan is not a target to steer by.

**Where this lives:** the goal itself in the tracker, at the top of the backlog. This document, holding the measures and the assumptions, in the wiki beside the rest of the product context.
